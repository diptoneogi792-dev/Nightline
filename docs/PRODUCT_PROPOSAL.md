# Product proposal

## Concepts considered

### Nightline

An anonymous weekly support pulse for students that proves a valid response was classified once while keeping every exact answer and reflection local. Students, support teams, and course leads get an honest aggregate without building a sensitive-response database. Midnight is essential because classification, range checks, worker control, and uniqueness are verified without publishing inputs. Gemini explains the public proof policy and disclosure choices from public labels only. The visual direction is a Japanese editorial grid interpreted through Afrofuturist instruments: indigo-black space, precise rules, electric citron signals, persimmon annotations, and calm orbital motion.

### Margin

A private eligibility gate for emergency microgrants. Students prove that public criteria are met without exposing income, family circumstances, or disability documentation. The chain would reveal only eligible/ineligible and a one-use application nullifier; Gemini would convert public policy into a proof checklist. Its one-minute story is clear, but a responsible MVP needs trusted credential issuers and careful institutional policy integration.

### Quiet Desk

A sealed-bid allocation system for scarce studio desks and equipment time. Students submit private preference weights; the public result reveals winning allocations without exposing losing bids. Gemini would detect ambiguity in the public allocation rules. Midnight privacy is central, but a fair multi-party allocation circuit and deadline lifecycle are too broad for a fully tested MVP.

## Selection

Nightline won because the privacy benefit is immediate, the disclosure boundary fits on one screen, and the complete flow is demonstrable in under a minute: answer locally, connect 1AM, deploy a personal worker, prove one band, and view the finalized receipt. It also gives students useful agency without asking them to upload evidence or trust an AI with sensitive context.

## Student experience principles

- Lead with the task and privacy boundary before wallet mechanics.
- Use plain language for proofs, finality, DUST, and network errors.
- Keep private reflection available even when the wallet or API is offline.
- Never display generated or placeholder chain identifiers.
- Treat urgent aggregate signals as planning data, not an individual diagnosis.
